Real-Time Market Data Ingestion & Anomaly Detection

deployed 2026
  • Polls 15 crypto assets from CoinGecko every 30 seconds, with on-demand 30-day backfill and explicit 429 handling.
  • Idempotency enforced at the database, not the application: a unique constraint on (asset_id, fetched_at) plus ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING, so a replayed batch is a no-op.
  • Exponential-backoff retries across four attempts; ingestion and detection run in isolated failure paths so neither can cascade into the other.
  • Anomaly detection by z-score with a 2% minimum-move floor — which is what stops stablecoins from firing an alert on every tick — plus separate data-gap detection.

engineering notes — two bugs worth the story

The column that was too small. A String(20) field looked generous until real asset identifiers arrived longer than that and writes started failing. Widened to String(50). The lesson that stuck: schema limits picked from the examples in front of you are guesses, and they fail on the data you haven't seen yet.

The stablecoins that were always anomalous. Pure z-score detection flagged stablecoins constantly — their price barely moves, so the standard deviation is tiny and any normal tick looks like a huge deviation. Adding a 2% absolute minimum-move floor on top of the z-score killed the false positives without weakening detection on volatile assets.

engineering notes — why BM25 by hand

Writing the ranker instead of importing it. BM25 is implemented from scratch rather than pulled from a library, because the point of the project is understanding information retrieval, not assembling it. Term frequency saturation and length normalisation are much harder to hand-wave once you've had to tune them.

Hybrid over pure vector search. Dense embeddings miss exact identifiers — a function name, an error string — which is precisely what you search a codebase for. Keyword and vector results are fused with Reciprocal Rank Fusion, and the eval harness is what decides whether any of it actually helped.
